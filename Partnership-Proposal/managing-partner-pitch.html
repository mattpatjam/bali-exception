The Problem

BE is exposed, the numbers back it up

FazWaz is expanding aggressively. Bali Home Immo has real digital infrastructure behind it. BE still has no formal training system, new agents learn by shadowing whoever's around, and there's no repeatable process for turning one good salesperson into a second one. None of this is anyone's fault, BE grew fast on relationships and referrals. Relationships alone don't hold up against firms that no longer need them.

As zoning and licensing rules keep tightening and the market fills with agencies moving volume over quality, an undifferentiated inventory stops being a strength and starts being a liability.

Compensation

A floor, not a ceiling

This pays for time that doesn't look like a sale: in the office, training the team, building what's on the previous page, not out on the floor showing villas. The point isn't less production, it's multiplying what the whole team produces instead of just my own. Commission and profit share reward that on top of it.

ComponentStructure
Base salary$70K/year gross, fixed, paid monthly regardless of production. The floor.
Personal commissionStandard agent rate on deals closed personally. The salary acts as a draw: threshold is 50% of base salary ($35K/year). Commission below it is already covered, commission above it pays out on top. Example below.
Profit shareTracks whatever percentage of the company I own that year (see next page), applied to Bali Exception's net profit. Cash, paid annually, on top of equity.
Example, how the threshold works

At 12 personal deals/year (~$300K average sale, 5% commission, standard agent rate): $300,000 × 5% × 50% = $7,500/deal, ×12 = $90,000 in personal commission for the year.

Threshold is 50% of the $70K salary, $35,000. Since $90K clears $35K, the salary is paid as normal, plus the $55K above threshold, $125K between salary and commission alone. Below threshold, the $70K floor holds, nothing clawed back.

Equity, Earned Not Granted

20% combined, vested over 4 years

10% from Typhaine, 10% from Raphael. Even vesting, 5% a year, against a named KPI set reviewed jointly each year-end. Not a flat grant on day one. The profit share on the previous page tracks this same percentage each year, it's not a separate number, it's what I actually own, nothing more.

Ownership becomes cash through a buyout

Equity vests as a stake in the company, it doesn't pay out on its own, the profit share on the previous page is what does that annually. To turn the stake itself into cash: a put option lets me require Typhaine and Raphael to buy back my vested shares, paid in installments rather than a lump sum, not exercisable before Year 4-5, and priced against a multi-year EBITDA average rather than a single year's snapshot. They hold the mirror right if I leave.
