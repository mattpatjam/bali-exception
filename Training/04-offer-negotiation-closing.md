# Stage 4: Offer, Negotiation & Closing

**Objective:** move a genuinely interested buyer to a signed offer. This is the highest-skill stage, expect it to take the longest to certify on.

**Previous stage:** [03-viewings-property-matching.md](03-viewings-property-matching.md)
**Next stage:** [05-contracts-legal-process.md](05-contracts-legal-process.md)

*Sources: Sabri Suby, Alex Hormozi, cold outreach sales training reel (FRAME framework). Full reference: `.claude/knowledge/advertising/sales-closing.md`*

---

## Core Philosophy

Sales is not about convincing or pushing, it's about pulling. The buyer should arrive at the decision themselves, your job is to ask the right questions, remove resistance, and guide them to a conclusion they already sense is correct.

The higher the price, the more this applies. Villa and land deals are high-ticket, buyers here are logical, not impulsive. Adjust accordingly: closes that rely on emotion and urgency alone don't hold up two weeks later when the excitement fades and the wire transfer is due.

**Only use these tactics when you genuinely believe the property is right for this buyer.** Never push someone toward a deal that doesn't serve them.

---

## Opening the Call: FRAME

Use this to structure the offer/negotiation conversation itself, before dropping into the tactics below.

- **F — Frame.** Set expectations up front: what this call is for, roughly how long it'll take, what happens at the end (an offer, a next step, or a clear no). Removes ambiguity that otherwise makes the buyer defensive.
- **R — Read.** Assume, don't ask. You already have everything from discovery and the viewing, state their situation back to them confidently instead of re-asking generic questions.
- **A — Admit.** Lay out the problem or hesitation you're sensing, and get the buyer to say it out loud themselves. Same mechanism as "the admission" in Pull, Don't Push below, once they name it, they own it.
- **M — Match.** Match the property/terms directly to the problem they just admitted to. Not a generic pitch, the specific fix for what they actually said.
- **E — Engage.** Ask if they'd move forward, if it adds up for them. A soft, logical check, not a hard close, just testing whether the match lands before you go further.

## The 7 Tactics (Sabri Suby)

**1. Kill the Comfort Zone.** Don't navigate around a painful truth to keep the buyer comfortable. Ask the direct questions. Ask for the decision more than once, don't stop at the first hesitation.

**2. Radical Transparency.** Raise the 1-3 biggest drawbacks yourself, before the buyer can. ("This lease has 3 years left before renewal, here's what that means for you.") It disarms the objection and builds trust simultaneously, they stop wondering what you're not telling them.

**3. Pull, Don't Push.** Ask strategic questions, let the buyer arrive at the decision themselves.
- "What led you to start looking at Bali in the first place?"
- "How long have you been considering this?"
- "What have you looked at already, and why didn't it work?"

Once you understand their situation: "Based on everything you've told me, I think this is a good fit for you," not "This is perfect for you" before you've actually listened.

**4. Cost of Inaction.** The real risk isn't spending money, it's staying where they are. Reframe: "What does waiting another year cost you, at the rate this area is appreciating?" Time is the enemy, the longer they wait, the more the opportunity or price moves against them.

**5. Tonality.** Slower pace, flatter delivery, measured and calm, not upbeat and eager. You're assessing fit, not selling. That posture shift changes how you sound automatically.

**6. Vision Selling.** Sell the outcome, not the paperwork. Nobody wants a leasehold contract, they want the life the villa gives them. Ask: "What does owning this actually change for you?" Paint it vividly, then mention logistics briefly.

**7. Follow-Up.** The prospect didn't say no, they said "not yet." Most agents give up after 2 attempts. Keep a real follow-up cadence, not "just checking in" messages.

## Hormozi's Logical Closing Framework

**Opticals vs. objections:** opticals come up before you present the offer, objections come after. Handle as many opticals as possible during discovery and viewings, so by the time you present the offer, you're only dealing with real objections.

**The Power Framework, three layers, work through all of them:**

| Layer | What it sounds like | Objection type |
|---|---|---|
| Circumstances | "I can't afford it right now" / "the timing is off" | Time, money |
| Other people | "I need to talk to my spouse/partner" | Authority |
| Self | "I need to think about it" | Fear of making a mistake |

These are nested. Handling one doesn't mean the deal is closed, there may be a layer underneath.

### Time objection — "not the right time"

- "Do you think the market/your situation will be less busy or less complicated a year from now?"
- "What would actually have to be different for the timing to feel right?" — usually surfaces there is no "right time," the wait is indefinite

### Money objection — "I can't afford it"

- It's rarely actually about money, it's about perceived value. If the value is clear, resources stop being the real constraint
- "You're going to spend this money either way, on this property or on years of continuing to look. The question is where."
- Ask: "How many more comparable properties would you need to see before this felt like the right call?"

### Authority objection — "I need to talk to my spouse/partner"

1. Isolate the real objection: "What part of this do you think they'd be unsure about?" Their answer is usually their own hesitation, not the absent party's
2. Ask why: "Why do you think they wouldn't approve?" — surfaces the actual fear
3. Reframe: "Are you looking for permission, or are you looking for support?" Most people have been seeking permission to stay stuck

### Fear of mistake / "I need to think about it"

- The rocking chair test: "When you're back home tonight thinking about it, walk me through exactly what you'll be weighing." Most people can't answer, because they're not actually going to think it through, it's a deferral
- Present the decision criteria out loud: "Do you believe this property is a good fit? Do you trust the process? Do you have access to the funds? So what's actually left to decide?"

## The Directional Decision Frame

Replace "will this get me exactly what I want?" (impossible bar) with:

**"Will this get me closer to what I want, or further from it?"**

Closer, move forward. Further, don't. That's the only decision that needs to be made. Removes the pressure of a "perfect" choice.

## The Mirror Close

Reflect the buyer's stated obstacle back as the reason to move forward.
- "I don't have time to deal with this right now" → "That's exactly why it's worth locking in now, before it takes more of your time later."
- "My partner isn't sure" → "That's part of why getting clear terms in writing now matters, so you can show them exactly what this is."

## The Brick Principle

No single property or deal delivers everything. "Some past investments didn't work out completely, but they got you a step closer to where you want to be. This is another step." Use this for "it might not be perfect" hesitation.

## No Reason Left

At the end: "Walking away changes nothing, moving forward has real upside. If there's no real downside either way, which option actually gives you something?"

## A Few Mini-Closes Worth Knowing (Brunson)

Use during Q&A or when a buyer is close but stuck, not all at once.

- **Money is good** — money is a tool for exchange, reframe it as a vehicle, not a sacrifice
- **Money replenishes, time doesn't** — if they wait, the thing they actually lose is time, not money
- **Two choices** — walk them through both paths (buy now vs. keep looking) side by side, make the contrast concrete
- **Now and later** — paint their situation now vs. after the purchase, make the contrast visceral

## Checklist: Pre-Close

- [ ] All opticals (concerns raised before this point) already addressed, not carried into the close
- [ ] You genuinely believe this property is right for this buyer before pushing
- [ ] Tonality checked: calm, measured, not salesy
- [ ] Vision (the outcome) established before discussing terms

## Checklist: Objection Response

- [ ] Identified which layer the objection is on: circumstances, other people, or self
- [ ] Used the matching framework for that layer, not a generic response
- [ ] Didn't assume one objection handled means the deal is closed, checked for another layer underneath
- [ ] If still stuck, used the Directional Decision Frame to lower the bar to "closer or further"
- [ ] Logged the objection and how it was handled in the CRM, for pattern-spotting across deals
